Restoring Bangladesh's mangroves, one validated site at a time.

About our project

A science-first answer to a very local problem.

Bangladesh's mangrove belt — including the Sundarbans, the world's largest contiguous mangrove forest — is degrading under pressure from aquaculture expansion, deforestation, and rising salinity, just as climate change makes its coastal-protection role more critical than ever.

Rather than planting wherever land is available, this project builds a repeatable pipeline: screen the coast by satellite, confirm conditions on the ground, design restoration around what each site actually needs, and track recovery with open-source monitoring tools.

Our approach

Four phases, from satellite to seedling.

PHASE 01

Site Identification

Satellite observations and GIS-based suitability mapping to rank degraded coastal zones.

PHASE 02

Field Validation

Ground truthing and community consultation to verify what the satellites suggest.

PHASE 03

Restoration Planning

Ecologically appropriate, participatory restoration strategies designed site by site.

PHASE 04

Monitoring

Open-source geospatial tools tracking recovery long after planting is done.

Why this project exists

Our mission

To advance scientifically informed, community-centred mangrove restoration in coastal Bangladesh — integrating geospatial technology, field evidence, and local participation to strengthen ecological resilience and support sustainable coastal management.
